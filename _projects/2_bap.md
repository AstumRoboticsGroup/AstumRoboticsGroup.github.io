**Development of a Robust Controller-Based Autonomous Attitude Stabilization System Following Air-to-Air Separation from a
Carrier UAV**

_Taşıyıcı İHA'dan Hava-Hava Ayrılma Sonrasında Gürbüz Kontrolör Tabanlı Otonom Yönelim Stabilizasyon Sistemi Geliştirilmesi_

**Funding:** Necmettin Erbakan University Scientific Research Projects Coordination Office (BAP)<br>
**Principal investigator:** Dr. Ali Tahir Karasahin

A carrier platform can bring a smaller UAV to the mission area and release it in flight. This extends range and enables
low-observable and multi-mission operations. At the moment of air-to-air separation, however, the released UAV inherits the
carrier's linear velocity and angular momentum and starts to tumble. Its dynamic parameters (motor effectiveness, moment of
inertia and centre of gravity) change at separation, and the carrier's propeller downwash adds a strong disturbance. Under
these conditions, conventional model-based controllers struggle to recover the vehicle within the short window available.

This project develops a robust autonomous attitude stabilisation system based on Incremental Nonlinear Dynamic Inversion (INDI),
which relies little on a model of the vehicle, and explicitly accounts for the disturbances specific to air-to-air separation.
The system is first validated in simulation over a large set of randomised initial conditions, and then tested on real
hardware: on the group's existing single-axis test stand and on a three-degree-of-freedom gimbal test stand that will be
designed and built within the project.
